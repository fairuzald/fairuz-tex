\begin{center}
    \begin{singlespace}
        {\large\bfseries MODULAR KNOWLEDGE-BASE CONSTRUCTION FOR MULTI-DOMAIN OMNI-RAG: EVALUATING CHUNKERS, INDEXERS, AND A LEGAL KNOWLEDGE GRAPH}

        {\normalfont\normalsize By:}

        {\bfseries Moh Fairuz Alauddin Yahya\\
        Agung Dewandaru\\
        I Wayan Gunada}
    \end{singlespace}
\end{center}

\begin{singlespace}
    \small

    Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems must turn heterogeneous documents into searchable information units before a language model can produce an answer. This paper presents a modular approach based on a canonical representation and a common \emph{plugin} interface for knowledge-base construction in OMNI-RAG. The evaluation does not examine plugin internals; instead, it compares \emph{chunker}, \emph{indexer}, and \emph{knowledge-graph} strategies while changing one component at a time.

    The experiments use nine Netgear \emph{User Manuals} comprising 1,400 pages and 200 bilingual questions, together with a legal corpus containing 256 documents and 25 verified questions. At $K=30$, the \emph{structure-aware hybrid} configuration reaches a recall of 0.740 on the User Manual corpus, while \emph{structure-aware dense} reaches 0.785. On the legal corpus, \emph{legal-structure dense} reaches a recall of 0.6854, whereas \emph{structure-aware hybrid} achieves the highest MRR at 0.5439. A knowledge-graph traversal to depth two reaches a neighborhood recall of 1.0000 with the \emph{sliding} configuration.

    The results indicate that preserving document structure and matching semantics improve context coverage, but the best configuration depends on the domain and the metric being prioritized. They also show that a \emph{plugin} abstraction can serve as both a strategy-exchange boundary and an experimental control mechanism without changing the core orchestrator.

    \textit{\textbf{Keywords}:} OMNI-RAG, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, knowledge-base construction, chunking, indexer, knowledge graph, plugin architecture.
\end{singlespace}
